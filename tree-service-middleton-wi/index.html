The Good Neighbor City, and its very large trees

Middleton is older and leafier than most of its neighbors, which means bigger trees, closer houses and more careful work.

Downtown and the old neighborhoods. The streets around Parmenter Street, Hubbard Avenue and Elmwood Avenue carry maples, oaks and lindens that have been growing for the better part of a century. They shade whole blocks, and they hang over roofs, garages and the wires behind them. Almost nothing here can be felled in one piece. Trees come down from the top, a limb at a time, lowered on ropes.

The conservancy edge. Hundreds of Middleton back yards end at the Pheasant Branch Conservancy, Tiedeman Pond, Stricker’s Pond or a city greenway. That is a wonderful thing to live beside, and it means oaks and hickories on the lot line, dead ash leaning in from the public side, and buckthorn pushing under the fence.

The lake. Along the Mendota shore, cottonwoods and willows grow huge in wet ground and shed limbs in every storm. Access is usually a narrow side yard, and there is a house on one side and water on the other.

The newer west and north. Middleton Hills, Foxridge, Stonefield, Orchid Heights and the streets out toward Pleasant View have younger trees that are just reaching the size where structure matters. A little pruning now is what keeps them from becoming the next generation’s problem trees.

Middleton tree rules, and who to call

Middleton runs its own forestry program, separate from Madison’s. These are the points that affect a homeowner.

Street trees are the City’s

Trees inside the public road right-of-way, including the terrace between the sidewalk and the street, are owned and maintained by the City of Middleton. The City prunes and removes them. If one needs attention, call Forestry at 608-821-8380 or Public Works at 608-821-8370.

Yard trees are yours, including the part over the street

Trees on your lot are your responsibility, and we know of no general permit for removing or pruning them. Middleton does expect owners to keep private trees trimmed clear of the roadway; the figure in the city code, as we read it, is 13.5 feet above the street.

Conservancy and greenway edges

A tree on the far side of your back fence may belong to the City. Before work on a boundary tree along Pheasant Branch, a pond or a greenway, we confirm where the lot line is. A dead or leaning tree on public land is reported to the City, not cut by a neighbor.

Oaks follow the DNR calendar

Madison’s oak work permit does not reach Middleton, but oak wilt does. The Wisconsin DNR advises against pruning oaks from April through July. Middleton’s oak woods are worth protecting, so we prune oaks in the cold months and seal storm wounds at once.

The tree work Middleton calls us for

Mature trees, close quarters and a lot of public land next door shape most of what we do here.

  • Deadwood in mature trees. A big healthy oak or maple still sheds dead branches, and over a roof, a driveway or a play area those are worth removing before the wind does it.
  • Roof and street clearance. Limbs resting on shingles or hanging low over the road are lifted with a few well-placed cuts, never by topping.
  • Lakeshore cottonwoods and willows. Fast, soft-wooded and enormous. Long limbs are reduced to take weight off, and trees that have begun to hollow are taken down before the lake wind decides.
  • Dead ash along the conservancy. Emerald ash borer has been through all of Dane County. Ash on your side of the line is yours to deal with; ash on the public side gets reported.
  • Buckthorn coming under the fence. Cut, cleared and the stumps ground, so the same thicket does not grow back in three years.
  • Young trees in Middleton Hills and the newer west side. Structural pruning now, while the cuts are small, is the most valuable tree care those streets can get.

Tree service questions from Middleton

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Middleton?

We know of no general permit for removing a tree in your own Middleton yard. Trees in the road right-of-way, and on conservancy and greenway land, belong to the City, which does its own pruning and removal. If you’re unsure which you have, Forestry (608-821-8380) can tell you.

Who looks after the tree between my sidewalk and the street?

The City of Middleton. Trees within the public road right-of-way are city-owned, and the City prunes and removes them. Report a problem to Forestry at 608-821-8380 or Public Works at 608-821-8370. Please don’t prune a street tree yourself.

My yard backs onto Pheasant Branch. Whose trees are on the line?

It depends where the lot line actually runs, and fences are often not on it. We confirm the boundary before quoting. Trees on your side are yours. Trees on the conservancy side are the City’s, and a dead or leaning one should be reported to Middleton Forestry.

Can you take down a big tree between two houses?

Yes. That describes most of our Middleton work. The tree is dismantled from the top, each piece lowered on ropes to a drop zone, and the trunk brought down in short sections. Nothing free-falls, so roofs, fences and gardens underneath stay as they were.

Do Madison’s oak rules apply in Middleton?

No. Madison’s April 1 to October 15 oak work permit applies only inside the City of Madison. We follow the Wisconsin DNR’s advice in Middleton: no oak pruning from April through July, caution into fall, and any storm wound on an oak sealed immediately.

Do you work on lakeshore properties?

Yes. Shoreline jobs usually mean a narrow side yard, soft ground and a tree that can only go one way, so they are planned carefully. Waterfront lots can carry extra rules about clearing near the water, and we check with the City before removing trees at the lake edge.
