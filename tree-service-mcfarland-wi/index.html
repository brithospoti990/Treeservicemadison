A lake village and its trees

McFarland grew from a railroad village on Lake Waubesa into one of Dane County’s busiest suburbs, and you can read that history in its trees.

The village center. Around Exchange Street, Farwell Street and the Municipal Center on Milwaukee Street, the houses and the trees are the oldest in town. Silver maples, oaks and big spruce overhang small lots, and removals are done in pieces, lowered on ropes.

The Waubesa shore. McFarland began as a lake community, with resorts along the east shore, and the shoreline lots still run deep and narrow from the road to the water. Cottonwoods, willows and old oaks stand between tightly spaced houses, and the ground at the lake end is soft. Near Babcock County Park and the Yahara River, the low ground grows the same big, soft-wooded trees.

The newer neighborhoods. McFarland grew quickly once the Beltline made the commute easy, and it is still growing to the east and north, out toward Siggelkow Road. The trees there are young and mostly builder-planted, with leftover fencerow boxelder, mulberry and dead ash along the back lot lines.

Parks and mounds. Indian Mound Park protects the Lewis Mound Group, and Orchard Hill Park and McDaniel Park anchor their neighborhoods. Trees in those parks, and along village streets, belong to the Village, which runs its own forestry program.

Tree services we bring to McFarland

Five services, one crew. In McFarland the question is usually how to get the wood out.

Tree Removal

Village-center and shoreline trees come down in pieces, on ropes, and are carried out through narrow side yards. Newer neighborhoods often have room for a quicker, simpler takedown.

Tree Trimming & Pruning

Deadwood and end-weight in old maples and oaks, clearance over roofs and over the street, and early structural pruning for the young trees in the newer subdivisions.

Stump Grinding

After a removal, or for the fencerow stumps a builder left behind. We file the Diggers Hotline ticket and ask about private lines: on lake lots that often means pumps, lighting and irrigation.

Stump Removal

For additions, garages, patios and fence lines, where buried wood would let the ground settle later. Stump and main roots out, hole backfilled and compacted.

Emergency Tree Service

Lake wind and soft ground tip big trees over whole. We make the site safe, deal with the root plate properly, and document everything for your insurer. Wires go to the electric utility first.

McFarland tree rules, and who to call

McFarland is a village with its own code and its own forestry staff. Madison’s tree ordinances do not apply here.

Street and park trees are the Village’s

McFarland’s Public Works department looks after more than 4,000 public trees along streets and in parks. Work on a public tree needs the Village’s permission, so a dead or damaged street tree is reported to Public Works, not pruned by the neighbor or by us.

Yard trees are yours, including what hangs over the street

Trees on your lot are your responsibility. The village code, as we read it, expects owners to keep branches clear of the street to a height of about 13.5 feet. We know of no permit for ordinary removals or pruning in a private yard.

The lake edge

Shoreline lots can carry extra rules about clearing trees and vegetation close to the water. Before removing a tree at the lake end of a Waubesa lot, we check with the Village. Pruning, deadwood removal and storm cleanup are rarely affected.

Oaks follow the DNR calendar

There is no Madison-style oak permit for private trees here, but oak wilt is present across Dane County. The Wisconsin DNR advises against pruning oaks from April through July. We prune oaks in the cold months and seal storm wounds immediately.

The tree work McFarland calls us for

A lake on one side and new construction on the other make for a varied week.

  • Shoreline cottonwoods and willows. Huge, soft-wooded and rooted in wet ground. Long limbs are reduced to take weight off them, and hollow or leaning trees are taken down in pieces before a storm does it badly.
  • Whole trees tipped over. Saturated soil and lake wind pull root plates out of the ground. A tipped tree is dangerous to cut, because the root plate can drop back into its hole when the trunk is freed. Please leave those to us.
  • Old maples in the village center. Deadwood, weak forks and decay from old storm wounds. Pruning first; removal when the trunk can no longer be trusted.
  • Dead ash on back lot lines. Emerald ash borer has been through all of Dane County. Untreated ash are dead or dying and turn brittle quickly.
  • Young trees in new neighborhoods. Double leaders and low, crowded limbs corrected while the cuts are still small. It is the most valuable tree care a young tree gets.
  • Fencerow clearing. Boxelder, mulberry and buckthorn cleared from the back of the lot, with the stumps ground so they do not resprout.

The Village makes wood from its own forestry work available to residents, which tells you how seriously McFarland takes its trees. For storm damage on private property, start with our emergency tree service page or call (608) 571-1326.

Tree service questions from McFarland

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in McFarland?

We know of no permit for removing an ordinary tree in your own yard. Street and park trees belong to the Village, and work on them needs the Village’s permission. Shoreline lots can carry extra rules about clearing near the water, so we check before quoting a removal at the lake edge.

Who looks after the tree between my sidewalk and the street?

The Village of McFarland. Public Works manages more than 4,000 public trees, including street trees, and work on them needs the Village’s permission. Report a dead, damaged or low-hanging one to Public Works. Private crews, ours included, don’t prune or remove them.

A tree on my lake lot has tipped over with the roots in the air. Is it safe to cut?

No. The weight of the trunk is holding the root plate up. When the trunk is cut free, the plate can slam back into its hole with enough force to kill someone standing in it. Keep children and pets away, and call us at (608) 571-1326.

Can you get equipment down to the lake end of a narrow lot?

Usually. We plan the route at the estimate: gate widths, steps, soft ground, septic covers and anything that can’t take weight. Where a machine can’t go, the wood is cut small and carried out by hand, and that is reflected in the written quote.

Do Madison’s oak rules apply in McFarland?

No. Madison’s April 1 to October 15 oak work permit applies only inside the City of Madison. We follow the Wisconsin DNR’s advice in McFarland: no oak pruning from April through July, caution into fall, and storm wounds on oaks sealed immediately.

Which utility do I call about a tree on a power line?

Call 911 first if a line is down or sparking, then the electric utility named on your bill. In this part of Dane County that is Alliant Energy (800-255-4268) or MGE (608-252-7000). No tree crew can touch the tree until the utility has made the line safe.
