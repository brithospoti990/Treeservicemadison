Tree Guides for Madison, WI Homeowners

Madison has more tree rules than most cities, and most of them are sensible once somebody explains them. These tree guides do that in plain English, with the ordinance numbers, the phone numbers and the dates, checked against the City’s and the State’s own pages.

Four rules worth knowing

  • Trees in your yard are yours. Trees on the terrace are the City’s.
  • Oaks: a free City permit is needed for any work from April 1 to October 15.
  • Ash: treat a healthy one, remove a failing one, and do not wait on a dead one.
  • Stumps: Diggers Hotline (811) must be called three working days before grinding.

The one date range to remember

If you take a single fact away from these tree guides, make it this one.

Between April 1 and October 15, any work on an oak inside the City of Madison needs a free permit from Urban Forestry, and that includes oaks in private back yards. The rule exists because fresh cuts on oaks attract the beetles that carry oak wilt, a disease that kills red oaks in a single summer and then spreads to their neighbors through the roots. The safe season for oak pruning is October 16 to March 31. The details are in the guide to oak wilt and the oak work permit.

Tree terms, in plain English

The words that turn up in quotes, ordinances and conversations with an arborist.

  • Terrace. The strip between the sidewalk and the curb. Public right-of-way, so the tree in it belongs to the City.
  • Right-of-way. Public land along a street. Where there is no sidewalk it can still reach 10 to 13 feet back from the pavement.
  • Certified arborist. Someone who has passed the International Society of Arboriculture’s exam and keeps up continuing education. Wisconsin has no state license for tree work, so this credential and proof of insurance are what to check.
  • ANSI A300. The national standard for pruning and other tree care. It rules out topping, flush cuts and spikes on living trees.
  • Branch collar. The slightly swollen ring where a branch meets the trunk. A correct pruning cut sits just outside it, because that tissue seals the wound.
  • Topping. Cutting a tree’s top or limbs back to stubs. It starves the tree, invites decay and produces weak regrowth. No good crew does it.
  • Crown reduction. The correct way to make a tree smaller: shortening limbs back to side branches large enough to take over.
  • Deadwood. Dead branches in a living crown. Removing them is the most common pruning visit and the one that does most for safety.
  • Root flare. Where the trunk widens into the roots. It should be visible at ground level. Buried under soil or mulch, it rots.
  • Tree cabling. Steel or synthetic cable installed high in a crown to support a weak fork. Specialist work, and sometimes the alternative to removal.
  • Stump grinding. Chipping a stump away to below ground level, leaving the roots to decay. Different from stump removal, which takes the main roots out too.
  • Root plate. The slab of roots and soil that lifts out of the ground when a tree blows over. Dangerous to cut near, because it can drop back into its hole.
  • Oak wilt. A fungal tree disease, fatal to red oaks, spread by beetles to fresh wounds and through shared roots.
  • Emerald ash borer. The beetle whose larvae have killed most of the Midwest’s untreated ash trees. In Madison since 2013.

Quick questions

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Madison?

Usually not, if the tree is in your own yard. The exceptions are any oak between April 1 and October 15, which needs a free City permit, and trees on the terrace, which belong to the City and are not yours to remove at all.

Who is responsible for the tree between the sidewalk and the street?

The City of Madison. Terrace trees are public property. Urban Forestry prunes, removes and replants them, and nobody else may work on them without a City permit. Report a problem at 608-266-4816.

When can oak trees be pruned in Madison?

From October 16 through March 31 without a permit. From April 1 to October 15 the City requires a free permit for any oak work, and the Wisconsin DNR advises against pruning oaks from April through July because of oak wilt.

Should I treat or remove my ash tree?

If the crown is still full, treatment by a certified applicator can protect it. If about half the crown is gone, plan the removal now. If the tree is dead, do not wait: dead ash becomes brittle quickly and is far harder to take down safely.

Does Wisconsin license tree services?

No. Wisconsin has no state license for tree work. What you can check is proof of insurance, and for diagnosis or treatment an ISA Certified Arborist credential, which can be verified at treesaregood.org. Pesticide applicators must be certified by the state.
